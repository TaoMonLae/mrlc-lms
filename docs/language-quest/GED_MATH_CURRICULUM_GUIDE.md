# GED Mathematical Reasoning curriculum guide

## Purpose and scope

`GED Mathematical Reasoning Preparation & Practice` is an independently authored MRLC Language Quest course. It contains 4 units, 40 concept-first lessons, and 120 original multiple-choice questions. It is preparation material, not an official GED Testing Service product, and it does not reproduce secure or official test questions.

The course teaches both mathematical content and the reasoning practices needed to select a model, use a formula, estimate, calculate, and interpret a result. Every lesson supplies a readable typeset formula, a worked example, key terms, a GED strategy, a checkpoint, hints, and answer explanations.

## Research method

The curriculum was mapped from first-party GED Testing Service publications available in August 2026:

- [GED Mathematical Reasoning Assessment Guide for Educators](https://www.ged.com/content/dam/websites/ged/resources/en/assessment-guide-for-educators-math.pdf) — assessment targets, reporting-category weights, mathematical practices, item formats, and formula sheet.
- [GED test subjects overview](https://api.ged.com/about_test/test_subjects/) — current test duration, two-part structure, calculator access, formula sheet, and item formats.
- [GED Mathematical Reasoning curriculum page](https://www.ged.com/en/curriculum/math.html) — official summary of concepts, measurements, equations, and real-world applications.
- [GED Mathematical Reasoning study guide](https://www.ged.com/content/dam/websites/ged/resources/GED-Study-Guide-Math.pdf) — first-party topic descriptions and public practice examples used only to understand expected task style.
- [GED High Impact Indicators](https://www.ged.com/content/dam/websites/ged/resources/High-Impact-Indicators.pdf) — priority skills that support multiple assessment targets.

Research extracted the official skills and weights, grouped related targets into a teachable sequence, and then wrote new MRLC explanations and questions. No third-party answer bank or official sample wording was copied.

## Blueprint alignment

The official assessment guide assigns approximately 45% of the test to quantitative problem solving and 55% to algebraic problem solving. It also defines four reporting categories. The 40-lesson course represents those categories exactly:

| Official reporting category | Official weight | Course unit | Lessons | Course share |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Quantitative Problem Solving with Rational Numbers | 25% | Unit 1 | 10 | 25% |
| Quantitative Problem Solving in Measurement, Data, and Probability | 20% | Unit 2 | 8 | 20% |
| Algebraic Problem Solving with Expressions and Equations | 30% | Unit 3 | 12 | 30% |
| Algebraic Problem Solving with Graphs and Functions | 25% | Unit 4 | 10 | 25% |

This also preserves the 18 quantitative and 22 algebraic lesson split, or 45%/55%.

## Formula readability and accessibility

Math content uses explicit `$...$` LaTeX delimiters and the shared KaTeX renderer. The same subject-aware renderer is used in lesson prompts, choices, hints, explanations, final exams, mastery reviews, heart refills, and boss battles. Long formulas can scroll horizontally rather than clipping, and course data does not rely on emoji or audio to communicate meaning.

The official formula sheet remains a learner resource; the course teaches how to select a formula rather than treating memorization as the primary goal. Calculator strategy is taught alongside estimation because calculator access does not replace modeling or reasonableness checks.

## Assessment design

- Three original questions per lesson, totaling 120.
- Four unique choices and exactly one keyed answer per question.
- Hints guide setup without disclosing the answer.
- Explanations show the governing relationship and calculation.
- All questions are eligible for the monitored Language Quest final-exam pool.
- Automated tests enforce lesson weights, unique prompts, content limits, visible math delimiters, absence of emoji/audio clues, concept parsing, and answer-key validity.

Language Quest currently expresses these items as accessible selected-response tasks. The official exam also uses fill-in-the-blank, drop-down, hot-spot, and drag-and-drop interactions; learners should use official GED Ready and GED practice materials to become familiar with those interfaces.

## Limitations and maintenance

- This course is a structured preparation path, not a prediction of a learner's GED score.
- It cannot reproduce the full adaptive feel, timing pressure, calculator interface, or every technology-enhanced item type of the official test.
- Public specifications may change. Recheck the linked first-party sources before revising weights, timing, calculator guidance, or target coverage.
- Review authored math after renderer changes on narrow and wide screens, in both light and dark themes.
- Keep currency symbols escaped or written as words so dollar signs remain unambiguous LaTeX delimiters.
