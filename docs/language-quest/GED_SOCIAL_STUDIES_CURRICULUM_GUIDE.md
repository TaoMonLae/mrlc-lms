# GED Social Studies Preparation & Practice

## Purpose

This guide documents the research basis, scope, design decisions, and quality limits of the original MRLC Learning Quest course `MRLC-GED-SOCIAL-STUDIES-V1`.

The course prepares learners to reason with social studies sources; it is not a memorization survey and does not reproduce official GED questions. It contains 4 units, 40 guided lessons, and 120 original four-option practices with hints, answer explanations, quantitative displays, and a monitored final exam.

## Research question and method

**Question:** What content and reasoning practice should a comprehensive Learning Quest course include to prepare learners for the current GED Social Studies assessment?

Research prioritized first-party GED Testing Service documents, then authoritative U.S. government sources for stable civic, historical, economic, and geographic concepts. Sources were checked for provenance, current availability, internal consistency, and relevance to an adult high-school-equivalency course. Official sample questions were not copied or adapted.

## Verified design requirements

- The official assessment is 70 minutes with no scheduled break. It permits the TI-30XS or built-in virtual calculator and includes multiple-choice and technology-enhanced items.
- The three broad reasoning areas—reading/writing, social studies concepts, and mathematical reasoning—are each approximately one-third of the assessment.
- Content weighting is approximately 50% civics and government, 20% U.S. history, 15% economics, and 15% geography and the world.
- Each assessed item combines a Social Studies Practice with a content topic. Practices include inference, central ideas, chronology, cause and effect, purpose and point of view, integrating text and visuals, evaluating evidence, comparing sources, interpreting graphs, and measuring center.
- Approximately half of official items use a shared stimulus for two or three questions. Learning Quest approximates this through a concept/source display followed by three related practices per lesson.
- GED Testing Service identifies central ideas/evidence, connections among people and processes, multiple causation, historical context and point of view, and cross-source comparison as high-impact instructional priorities.

## Curriculum map

| Unit | Official share | Lessons | Coverage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Civics and Government | 50% | 20 | Sources, democratic forms, natural rights, constitutionalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, federalism, branches, amendments, civil liberties, participation, parties, elections, Electoral College, policy, bias, and paired sources |
| United States History | 20% | 8 | Founding documents, early republic, expansion and Indigenous policy, slavery and Civil War, Reconstruction, suffrage and civil rights, world wars, Cold War and post-9/11 foreign policy |
| Economics | 15% | 6 | Scarcity, opportunity cost, supply and demand, market structure, incentives, labor and capital, productivity, trade, GDP, inflation, unemployment, fiscal and monetary policy, credit, and consumer decisions |
| Geography and the World | 15% | 6 | Maps and scale, human–environment interaction, resources, regions, borders, migration, cultural diffusion, population, urbanization, sustainability, and integrated data reasoning |

Every lesson supplies a concept summary, three objectives, two instructional explanations, a structured visual, key terms, a GED strategy, a checkpoint, and three unique scored questions. Civics lessons intentionally carry half of the course weight.

## Quality controls and limitations

- Unit proportions and structural rules are enforced by automated tests.
- Every answer has one correct option, three distinct distractors, a hint, and an explanation; lesson and option emojis are absent.
- Subject courses bypass language-only listening, spelling, and vocabulary-card stages.
- The course teaches broadly applicable reasoning and representative content. It cannot predict a learner’s official score or reproduce every interactive item type available on the official testing platform.
- Government, policy, economic data, and GED procedures can change. Maintainers should recheck the first-party sources during major revisions.
- GED and GED Testing Service are registered marks of their owners. This independently authored course is not an official GED product and is not endorsed by GED Testing Service.

## AI-assisted research disclosure

AI-assisted research and drafting tools were used to locate sources, synthesize the official blueprint, draft original instructional material, and perform consistency checks. Maintainers remain responsible for factual review, classroom suitability, source verification, and future updates.
