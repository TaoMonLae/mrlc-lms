# Student exam walkthrough · 5 November 2026

The open Brave tab showed the GED RLA Final Exam in Studio’s student preview. Question 21 was the extended response in a 53-question paper. I moved to question 22 and back to 21 in that preview. The follow-up checks used simulated attempts in a separate local copy.

## Reading and writing

Keeping the passage beside the writing area lets a student consult evidence without leaving the question. On the laptop screen, the essay directions filled the right pane. Focusing the writing box took the directions out of view. A student who wanted to check the assignment again had to scroll back through the pane, then find their draft.

Long essay questions now offer **Go to response** and **Read prompt again**. Each moves keyboard focus to its destination. The desktop header and progress row take up less space, and the initial writing box fits a shorter screen. Students can still resize it.

## Moving between questions

In the open preview, the scrollbar covered part of the navigator’s rightmost column. The desktop passage layout now leaves room for the scrollbar and keeps the question label and status key outside the scrolling numbers. Next and Previous keep the active number in view within the navigator, including the horizontal strip on a phone.

## Reviewing and submitting

A student who opened review used to pass all 53 rows to reach the return and finish buttons. Those actions now sit above the list. If the submission warning identifies missing answers, **Go back and answer** opens the first unfinished question. Final submission still requires a separate confirmation.

## Timed essays and breaks

The live player used to announce every essay-clock tick and word-count change to screen readers. Students can still read both values, and the existing time warnings remain available. The break screen also used to say answers were saved while a save could still be pending. A failed save now shows a retry button during the break.

The Studio preview lets teachers explore questions without saving an attempt. It shows the overall countdown but does not simulate a configured essay deadline or break. Those behaviors require a student-player check.

Eight local Brave checks passed across desktop and emulated phone sizes. They covered the 53-question essay walkthrough, preview answer and flag handling, student submission confirmation, and retrying a failed essay save during a break. These checks used simulated server responses.
