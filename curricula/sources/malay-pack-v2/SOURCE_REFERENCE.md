## How the source was used

The 1943 guide supplies the practical sequence and communication goals: pronunciation practice, greetings, location, directions, numbers, identifying objects, asking for things, shopping, money, time, reusable sentence frames, public signs, and thematic vocabulary.

This course is **not a transcription or facsimile**. It is newly written for current learners. The adaptation:

1. converts old spellings to current standard Malaysian Malay;
2. removes military commands, wartime assumptions, tobacco purchasing, and dated social labels;
3. replaces colonial-era geographic and demographic claims with no such claims;
4. uses civilian situations in Malaysia: school, markets, restaurants, transport, hotels, clinics, public services, and community life;
5. treats old forms only as historical recognition activities, never as the preferred modern answer;
6. uses `audioText` for every answer so learners can listen and repeat with an installed `ms-MY` browser/system voice.

## Historical caution

The primary source was produced for U.S. military personnel in 1943 and reflects that period's spelling systems, wartime purposes, colonial terminology, regional mixture, and cultural assumptions. It should be taught as a historical source, not as an authoritative description of present-day Malaysia or present-day standard Malay.
